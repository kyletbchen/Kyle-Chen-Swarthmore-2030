Personal Project

Green Hydrogen for Cargo Ships

Electrolysis Renewable Energy Prototyping Robotics

Overview

Shipping is one of the hardest sectors to decarbonize, which made it an interesting case study for renewable fuel. I researched how electrolysis splits water into hydrogen and oxygen using an electric current, and investigated green hydrogen's potential as a fuel for vehicles, comparing it against other renewable energy sources already in use.

To make the idea concrete, I built a small prototype system for a robotics competition that modeled how a cargo ship or port facility could use onboard solar power to electrolyze seawater into hydrogen fuel for industrial use — a scaled-down version of a shipboard fuel-production system. In the setup, a solar panel powers an anode and cathode submerged in water, and the reaction produces visibly measurable volumes of oxygen and hydrogen gas at each electrode. From there, I analyzed the broader sustainability case for green hydrogen, both as a maritime fuel and as a potential fuel source for cars, trains, and planes.
